How I read the numbers

Accounting is the story of how a business really runs. I read it in four layers.

1

The flow: AP, AR and Bank

These three pillars show where cash truly comes from and where it goes. When they are clean and reconciled, everything built on top can be trusted.

2

The close

Reconciliations, accruals and adjustments, the trial balance and the month-end close turn daily activity into a reliable, auditable picture. A good close is fast, repeatable and leaves a clear trail.

3

The package

P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, KPIs and forecast, reconciled to each other so the numbers tell one consistent story.

4

Tailored for management

A report only matters if management can act on it. I shape the information around the decisions in front of them, and always ask: what would someone do differently because of this number?

When a number looks wrong, I follow it back to the process that produced it. Most errors are process problems before they are accounting problems.

Four case studies

Each one starts with a process that was costing time or accuracy, and ends with something measurable.

Billing changes formintake and traceability
Problem

Change requests reached the billing team incomplete and untraceable, which meant rework and long email threads.

What I built

A structured intake form with a named owner and a timestamp on every step, automated follow-ups, and one log of every change.

Result

About 63% fewer maintenance hours and about 89% fewer emails, comparing the latest month with the earlier average. Each change now takes about 8 minutes instead of about 15. The billing cycle closes about 50% faster.

Financial package, automatedIn development

The financial package was built by hand every cycle, with version conflicts between Excel and HTML copies.

What I am building

Process restructuringa new way of working for almost 300 people
Problem

Processes were undocumented, work was duplicated and ownership was unclear across teams.

What I did

Mapped and defined the processes, wrote procedure packs and instructions, and set controls with named owners. The change touched the working practices of almost 300 people.

The hard part

Getting almost 300 people to change how they work. After two weeks of initial resistance, the new procedures were fully adopted, and accountability across different areas followed.

Result

Processes that are documented, followed and measurable.

Data normalisationone source of truth for AR
Problem

There was no single source of truth for AR data. Billing, collections and projections each kept their own version.

What I did

Normalised the data model so that three shared sources feed the three AR pillars: pre-billing, billing and collections. That makes dashboards and automation possible.

Insight

AR now runs for 200+ clients every period, all from the same data model.
